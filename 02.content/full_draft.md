Specialist Disability Accommodation, or SDA, is an emerging asset class. The New
Build classification began on 1 April 2016, so data on New Build dwellings has
existed for only around a decade; granular data is limited and, in many instances,
not publicly available, and public comparable-sale evidence remains limited. A
common source of value leakage is treating an SDA appointment as a conventional
property realisation — because an SDA appointment is not one asset. It is two.

**A. The SDA freehold property.** Its value can depend on SDA in-use economics, the
underlying alternative-use property value, participant funding, occupancy quality,
location, design category, building type, fire sprinkler configuration, remaining SDA
pricing tenure, provider arrangements and buyer or investor mandates.

**B. The SDA provider business or provider rights.** Distinct from the freehold asset.
Its economics and risks can involve provider registration, participant relationships,
service and management agreements, claiming and administration, tenancy and provider
transition, contractual rights and obligations, current sustainable cash flow, work in
progress / participant pipeline, brand / reputation value, and geographical reach. This
report does not attempt a full provider-business valuation — the point is narrower: an
appointee needs to know these are not interchangeable assets before assuming either
one's position for the other.

Because the class is nascent, SDA in-use value and alternative-use value can diverge
significantly, in either direction. Sometimes that gap reflects a fundamental
impairment. Sometimes it reflects a remediable chokehold. Specialist commercial
diagnosis is what tells the difference — this report explains why, not how to perform
that diagnosis yourself.

This report is a companion to *SDA Market Report 2026*, which provides commercial
insight into the SDA asset class's first decade. This report does not repeat that
material — it focuses on administration, distress, recovery and realisation for an
individual appointment.

This report assumes property, restructuring and lending expertise — not existing SDA
sector knowledge. The terms below recur throughout the report and are defined here for
quick reference.

**SDA / NDIS**
- **SDA** — Specialist Disability Accommodation: housing funded under the NDIS for
  participants with extreme functional impairment or very high support needs.
- **NDIS** — National Disability Insurance Scheme: the national scheme that funds
  supports for eligible people with disability, including SDA.
- **NDIA** — National Disability Insurance Agency: the Commonwealth agency that
  administers the NDIS and enrols and pays for SDA.
- **Enrolled Dwelling** — a dwelling formally enrolled with the NDIA as meeting SDA
  requirements, making it eligible to attract SDA payments.
- **SDA Provider** — an organisation registered to deliver, and claim payment for, SDA
  at an enrolled dwelling.

**Design Categories**
- **HPS** (High Physical Support), **FA** (Fully Accessible), **IL** (Improved
  Liveability) and **Robust** — four specialised SDA design categories, each addressing
  different physical, sensory or behavioural-safety needs.
- **Basic** — SDA design category for housing without specialised design features but
  with other SDA characteristics. Basic is available for Existing Stock, not New Build
  dwellings.

**Housing / Support**
- **SIL** — Supported Independent Living: NDIS-funded daily-living support, funded and
  delivered separately from SDA.
- **RRC** — Reasonable Rent Contribution: the tenancy-style rent a resident pays a
  provider, separate from and in addition to SDA payments.

**Property / Economics**
- **New Build** — SDA classification for dwellings meeting the applicable New Build
  criteria, including the relevant certificate-of-occupancy requirements from 1 April
  2016. **Existing Stock** — SDA classification for a dwelling that does not meet the
  New Build definition, priced on a different basis.
- **NOI** — Net Operating Income: income remaining after operating costs, before debt
  service and capital items.
- **CAPEX** — Capital Expenditure: capital-works spending, distinct from routine
  operating costs.
- **Vendor Due Diligence (VDD)** — a vendor's own pre-sale investigation of an asset's
  position; this report's central concept, Specialist SDA Commercial Vendor Due
  Diligence, is the SDA-specific version of it.

**Geography / Market**
- **SA4** — Statistical Area Level 4: an ABS geography classification commonly used to
  describe SDA supply and demand by region.

# PAGE 05 — WHAT THE HEADLINE DEMAND DATA DOES NOT TELL YOU

**v1.0 precision QA (2026-09-29):** the highlight-metric label and footnote were

As at 30 June 2026 — the latest official dataset verified for this report — 16,644
active participants had SDA funding in use, and a further 9,014 were assessed as
eligible for SDA but not yet using it. Nationally, 14,235 dwellings were enrolled as
SDA, representing an estimated 31,065 enrolled SDA places (see below); enrolled
dwellings grew from 7,925 in June 2023 to 14,235 in June 2026, and annualised SDA
supports over the same period rose from $365m to $684m.

**Eligibility, or the existence of SDA funding, does not by itself establish
commercially viable demand for a particular enrolled dwelling.** The quarterly data is
not granular enough for an investor to tell, from the headline eligible-not-using
count, whether an individual participant's funding level is commercially aligned with a
specific dwelling's enrolled pricing capacity. A participant can technically hold SDA
funding at a level materially below the enrolled income capacity of the dwelling they
wish to occupy.

The consequence: headline eligible-not-using volume may overstate economically
addressable demand for some properties, and a physically occupied place can still be
materially underfunded relative to its enrolled pricing capacity. Some of these
situations can be remediated; others cannot — identifying which is a diagnosis, not an
assumption either way.

NDIA quarterly reporting has separated "eligible, not yet using" from "using"
participants only since the Q4 2024-25 (30 June 2025) release. Across the four quarters
this split has been published, the count has moved from 9,880 (June 2025) to 9,577
(December 2025) to 9,370 (March 2026) to 9,014 (June 2026) — a 12-month decline, not a
static three-year pattern. From 27 August 2026, new plan-reassessment requirements
apply; the effect on individual SDA funding outcomes should not be inferred from this
change alone.

# PAGE 06 — WHAT MUST LINE UP FOR AN SDA ASSET TO WORK

**Reader question:** What am I dealing with?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 5 — shifts to page 6. Content unchanged; the
Alignment Chain is unaffected by round 2's feedback. See
`../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full old→new page map.

The chain below is what has to hold together for an SDA dwelling to convert into
transaction value. Enrolment alone does not create funding. Funding alone does not
create occupancy. Occupancy alone does not create claimable, collected cash.

The SDA Rules set these conditions out independently: a registered provider, an
enrolled dwelling, an eligible participant actually residing there, and alignment
between the dwelling's design category and the participant's plan. All must be
satisfied together — none substitutes for another.

A healthy asset shows alignment end to end. A distressed asset shows a break somewhere
in the chain — and the rest of this report is a method for finding exactly where.

# PAGE 09 — FROM MAXIMUM SDA PRICE TO ACTUAL CASH

**Reader question:** Is the revenue real?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 8 — shifts to page 9. Content unchanged besides
one cross-reference renumbering. See `../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full
old→new page map.

NDIS SDA pricing sets maximum prices for SDA supports — the NDIA describes them as
"appropriate and reasonable maximum prices," not guaranteed income, and its price
calculator is an expected-income tool, not proof that a specific dwelling will receive
that amount.

Getting from that headline price to cash in a property's account means the revenue has
to survive five more steps: funding attached to a specific participant, alignment
between that funding and this dwelling, actual physical occupancy, a claim correctly
lodged, and a claim actually paid and collected.

The SDA pricing framework does not remove the owner's commercial exposure to the
difference between theoretical pricing and realised property income — a gap that can
arise from funding, occupancy, claiming or provider execution, not only from the
pricing framework itself. Testing where that gap actually sits, asset by asset, is
core Commercial Due Diligence work before a vendor pathway is chosen.

This page stops at cash received. What that cash is actually worth once operating costs
are taken out is page 12's question, not this one.

# PAGE 10 — OCCUPIED DOES NOT NECESSARILY MEAN PERFORMING

**Reader question:** Why can an occupied SDA place still underperform economically?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 9 — shifts to page 10. See
`../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full old→new page map.

**Round 2 change type:** REVISED. Adds Steve's tenant-mix / short-term residential-use
observation, professionally hedged (not universal advice — "in some cases," "may,"
subject to the relevant tenancy/SDA/funding/contractual position). Sources tightened:
three source families acknowledged without padding — SDAHC Research (*SDA Market
Report 2026*) and a single combined NDIA citation (Supplement P + SDA Rules 2020),
rather than citing the same underlying NDIA dataset three times to appear more
evidenced than it is.

A dwelling can look fully occupied and still underperform economically. Physical
presence is not the same as funding, and funding is not the same as a lodged and
collected claim.

In practice this shows up as: funding that has not yet come through for a resident who
has moved in; funding that does not match this dwelling's design category; a
participant paying market rent rather than an aligned SDA arrangement; short-term,
non-SDA support propping up occupancy; or a claim sitting unlodged or unpaid.

Each of these produces the same headline number — "occupied" — while producing a
different income result. The gap between the two is usually where specialist diagnosis
finds the surprise.

In some cases the existing participant or tenant mix may itself constrain the
dwelling's in-use capital value. In other cases — particularly sole-occupancy
dwellings — a short-term residential tenancy strategy may be commercially preferable to
carrying prolonged vacancy while the longer-term SDA position is assessed, subject to
the relevant tenancy, SDA, funding and contractual position. This is a strategic option
to test, not a default recommendation — moving an SDA dwelling temporarily into the
general residential market is not necessarily frictionless.

Page 19 turns this into a question of who determines whether that occupancy actually
holds value. Here, the point is simpler: verify income, don't infer it from occupancy.

# PAGE 11 — THE PROVIDER, CONTRACTUAL AND PARTICIPANT INFORMATION LAYER

**Reader question:** Why is this information hard to see, and does that stop diagnosis?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 10 — shifts to page 11. See
`../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full old→new page map.

**Round 2 change type:** REVISED. Adds a paragraph on provider-change / re-enrolment
risk, per Steve's instruction — placed here (the provider/contractual layer) rather
than mechanically forced onto the NOI page, since it is fundamentally a contractual/
regulatory-timing issue, not an income-normalisation one. Uses current primary NDIA
guidance (a provider change cancels the existing dwelling enrolment; the incoming
provider must lodge a new application, treated as new even where the dwelling was
previously enrolled) rather than the weaker prior hedge ("can trigger an enrolment
review"). Cost range is stated as SDAHC experience, not invented figures.

SDA providers carry regulatory obligations under the SDA Rules — registration,
enrolment maintenance, and compliance with the Rules' service requirements. Layered on
top is a commercial agreement that allocates the economics between owner and provider.
That second layer is not incidental — it can materially affect control, transition
execution and saleability.

The relationship between owner, SDA provider, participants, participant funding,
SIL/support providers and tenancy documentation can also make relevant commercial
information genuinely difficult for an owner, lender or appointee to see clearly. That
is not, on its own, a reason to stop diagnosis. Relevant commercial information can
often be obtained and analysed in a de-identified manner that respects participant
privacy and choice and control — this report does not give privacy or legal advice on
how, only notes that the absence of direct participant detail should not automatically
be read as an absence of diagnosable information.

**A provider change carries its own timing risk, independent of the commercial
questions above.** Current NDIS guidance is specific: where a dwelling's existing SDA
provider stops managing it, that provider's enrolment of the dwelling is cancelled, and
the dwelling cannot support SDA payment claims until it is re-enrolled. The incoming
provider must submit a new enrolment application — treated as a new application even
where the dwelling was previously enrolled, not as a continuation or transfer of the
prior enrolment. The cost and work required can range from minimal to material,
depending on the dwelling, documentation, certification and issues identified during
the new enrolment process.

Twelve recurring commercial questions, evidenced across a working sample of SDA
agreements, cover this ground and can stand on their own as a commercial reference.

# PAGE 12 — FROM ACTUAL INCOME TO SUSTAINABLE NOI

**Reader question:** What does the asset actually earn on a sustainable basis?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 11 — shifts to page 12. Content unchanged
besides cross-reference renumbering. See `../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full

Accounting profit is not automatically transaction NOI. A sale-purpose reconciliation
starts from actual or annualised property income — not the headline price from page 9
— and works down through the costs of running the asset day to day: provider and
management fees, property outgoings (rates, insurance, water), routine repairs and
maintenance, and occupancy normalised across a period rather than read off a single
point in time. Non-recurring operating income or expenses are treated as normalisation
adjustments where appropriate, depending on their nature. The result is normalised,
sustainable NOI — not the accounting P&L, and not the maximum SDA price from page 9.

Sustainable NOI is not the same as post-CAPEX cash flow. Known capital expenditure or
capital works, capital reserves where adopted, and lease-up or stabilisation capital
requirements are considered separately from normalised NOI — they affect underwritten
earnings and price, but they are not routine operating costs.

No single percentage applies to any of these steps across every asset — each has to be
derived from the specific dwelling's own record, not assumed from a rule of thumb. This
reconciliation is precisely what the diligence process tests before a sale, not
something a headline P&L can substitute for.

# PAGE 13 — VALUE REFERENCES, REMAINING PRICING TENURE AND HIGHEST AND BEST USE

**Reader question:** Where is the value, and for how long does the current pricing
basis last?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 12 — shifts to page 13. Content unchanged; out
of scope for round 2's brief (New Build pricing-tenure exact duration remains
`PRIMARY SOURCE CONFIRMATION REQUIRED`, unchanged — see
`../../01.evidence/evidence_gap_resolution_round2.md` §7). See
`../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full old→new page map.

Two independently assessable value references frame the position for an SDA asset.

**SDA In-Use Value Reference** — a valuation of the asset operating under adopted
assumptions: occupancy, funding, provider performance and cost structure.

**Alternative-Use / Underlying Property Value Reference** — a valuation of the
underlying property under the relevant alternative-use assumptions.

Professional valuers assess these on different bases, and they can differ materially —
in either direction, depending on location, design and local demand. SDAHC has observed
cases where the SDA in-use valuation materially exceeds the underlying alternative-use
value, and cases where it does not. SDA in-use is not automatically the higher value:
some assets can recover substantial SDA value through remediation; others cannot, and
in those cases the highest and best use may ultimately be an alternative use. The
in-use reference is conditional on assumptions — design category, participant mix,
occupancy, provider arrangement, operating costs, lease-up allowance — that may not
hold in a distressed matter.

**Remaining New Build pricing tenure** can be a material value driver, worth naming on
its own terms and kept distinct from enrolment. SDA pricing classification distinguishes
New Build from Existing Stock treatment; where the applicable pricing framework
provides a finite New Build pricing period, the dwelling may transition to a different
(Existing Stock) pricing treatment once that period ends. This is a question about
pricing classification, not about how long the dwelling remains SDA-enrolled — the two
should not be conflated. The applicable pricing framework and rules should be checked
for the specific dwelling to confirm how much of its current New Build pricing period
remains and what happens once it is exhausted. A buyer may reasonably underwrite an
asset differently with materially more of that period remaining than with materially
less. This report does not assert an exact duration or start-date rule pending
primary-source confirmation, and does not model the capital-value effect of a specific
remaining term — that depends on the underlying alternative-use value and other
asset-specific assumptions — but appointees should treat remaining New Build pricing
tenure as a standing commercial due diligence item, not an afterthought.

**Current Recovery Position** — a commercial assessment of the current position,
reflecting actual occupancy, participant funding, provider arrangements, contractual
friction, required CAPEX, lease-up or stabilisation time, execution risk and holding
costs. Current Recovery Position is not a third independent valuation methodology.

Verify the assumptions behind both value references, and the remaining pricing tenure,
before relying on a valuation produced before the appointment began.

# PAGE 14 — WHAT EXACTLY IS IMPAIRED?

**Reader question:** What can go wrong, and how far does the diagnosis need to go?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 13 — shifts to page 14. Content unchanged besides
one cross-reference renumbering. See `../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full

A useful starting diagnosis is to test four potential sources of impairment.

**Operational** — the asset is enrolled and funded correctly, but day-to-day execution
is failing: claims aren't lodged, records are poor, vacancy isn't being actively
managed. **Provider / Contractual** — the provider relationship itself is the problem:
an unfavourable fee structure, unclear vacancy-risk allocation, a provider under
pressure, or a contract that blocks a clean transition. **Capital Structure** —
ownership, debt or security arrangements constrain recovery independently of, or in
addition to, operating performance. **Fundamental Asset** — location, design category,
configuration or realistic participant demand does not support the original SDA
proposition.

These four sources are a diagnostic starting point, not an exhaustive substitute for
Specialist SDA Commercial Vendor Due Diligence. Some engagements require a forensic
level of commercial investigation — testing for hidden commercial conflicts or
dependencies (related-party arrangements, undisclosed dependencies, participant/
provider incentives, cross-entity arrangements) rather than an accusation against any
identified party.

Identifying which source — or combination — applies determines the realistic recovery
pathway. This is the working diagnosis the decision tree on page 22 tests further.

# PAGE 21 - WHAT DUE DILIGENCE MAY REVEAL BEFORE REALISATION

**Reader question:** What may not be visible from the headline income position?

**Current income does not mean the future income stream is transaction-ready.** An enrolled, occupied SDA dwelling can appear sale-ready while carrying risks that interrupt income after sale. Other issues may represent recoverable value if identified before market.

Specialist SDA Commercial Vendor Due Diligence distinguishes durable income, remediable uncertainty and risks that should shape pricing, transaction conditions or the realisation pathway. A complete headline rent roll is a starting point, not sufficient evidence to launch. The four lenses below show what the process on page 20 may reveal.

Historic enrolment records past status; it does not replace confirmation of the current documentary and compliance position. Original design, certification gaps, missing historical documents or compliance issues may require investigation before market. Understanding that position early helps avoid remediable uncertainty being priced into the asset.

## ENROLMENT CONTINUITY

When a provider stops managing a dwelling, it must cancel enrolment. The incoming provider must apply afresh, even for a previously enrolled home, with the mandatory documents and relevant certifications.

A sale may prompt buyers, lenders or advisers to seek continuity evidence. In SDAHC’s market experience, some buyers may make satisfactory enrolment evidence a transaction condition.

**Confirm documentation and resolve remediable gaps before market.**

## OCCUPANCY / VACANCY INCOME

Participants may leave for individual reasons. Eligible vacancy payments can temporarily support income after a participant moves out of a qualifying shared home; eligibility, duration and evidence must be tested.

They do not cover newly built SDA homes that have never housed a participant.

**Test the income effect, any eligible claim and realistic replacement-occupancy timing.**

## FORENSIC COMMERCIAL REVIEW

A de-identified, forensic-style review may be needed to reconcile participant funding, occupancy, provider records, claims, agreements and cash receipts without unnecessarily exposing participant identities.

Identify which income is durable, which discrepancies can be remedied and which risks belong in the realisation strategy.

**Protect the opportunity for the strongest supportable realisation outcome.**

## TAX / GST / TRANSACTION STRUCTURE

**Freehold:** the current SDA pricing schedule distinguishes certain base amounts by historical GST and input-tax-credit treatment. Confirm the applicable treatment for each dwelling.

**Provider-business / management rights:** similar arrangements may have different GST treatment. Verify the supply, agreements and entity structure against applicable GST law with tax advisers. GST-free NDIS supplies require specific conditions; market practice is insufficient.

**Obtain specialist taxation advice.**

For SDA freehold interests or provider-business rights, first establish the commercial position, then test it with appropriate legal and taxation advisers before the agency appointment and market process are finalised. Tax treatment can materially affect transaction economics and structure. **Obtain specialist taxation advice before fixing the realisation strategy.** Page 22 translates the findings into pathway decisions.

**Sequence:** Commercial diagnosis → Legal / tax review where required → Realisation strategy → Specialist market process
