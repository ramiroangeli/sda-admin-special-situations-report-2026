Two independently assessable value references frame the position for an SDA asset.

**SDA In-Use Value Reference** — a valuation of the asset operating under adopted
assumptions: occupancy, funding, provider performance and cost structure.

**Alternative-Use / Underlying Property Value Reference** — a valuation of the
underlying property under the relevant alternative-use assumptions.

Professional valuers assess these on different bases, and they can differ materially —
in either direction, depending on location, design and local demand. SDAHC has observed
cases where the SDA in-use valuation materially exceeds the underlying alternative-use
value, and cases where it does not. SDA in-use is not automatically the higher value:
some assets can recover substantial SDA value through remediation; others cannot, and
in those cases the highest and best use may ultimately be an alternative use. The
in-use reference is conditional on assumptions — design category, participant mix,
occupancy, provider arrangement, operating costs, lease-up allowance — that may not
hold in a distressed matter.

**Remaining New Build pricing tenure** can be a material value driver, worth naming on
its own terms and kept distinct from enrolment. SDA pricing classification distinguishes
New Build from Existing Stock treatment; where the applicable pricing framework
provides a finite New Build pricing period, the dwelling may transition to a different
(Existing Stock) pricing treatment once that period ends. This is a question about
pricing classification, not about how long the dwelling remains SDA-enrolled — the two
should not be conflated. The applicable pricing framework and rules should be checked
for the specific dwelling to confirm how much of its current New Build pricing period
remains and what happens once it is exhausted. A buyer may reasonably underwrite an
asset differently with materially more of that period remaining than with materially
less. This report does not assert an exact duration or start-date rule pending
primary-source confirmation, and does not model the capital-value effect of a specific
remaining term — that depends on the underlying alternative-use value and other
asset-specific assumptions — but appointees should treat remaining New Build pricing
tenure as a standing commercial due diligence item, not an afterthought.

**Current Recovery Position** — a commercial assessment of the current position,
reflecting actual occupancy, participant funding, provider arrangements, contractual
friction, required CAPEX, lease-up or stabilisation time, execution risk and holding
costs. Current Recovery Position is not a third independent valuation methodology.

Verify the assumptions behind both value references, and the remaining pricing tenure,
before relying on a valuation produced before the appointment began.
