# PAGE 15 — HIDDEN CHOKEHOLDS: WHAT AFFECTS RECOVERABILITY

**Reader question:** What can go wrong, and is it fixable?

The Current Recovery Position for an SDA asset (page 13) is not fixed — it moves with a
number of factors, some within an appointee's control and some not. It does not
necessarily start at, or move toward, the SDA in-use value reference; the two are
related but distinct questions.

Not every constraint on recoverability is the same kind of problem. Some are
chokeholds — specific, identifiable and economically remediable before a sale. Others
are fundamental, and no reasonable remediation changes the underlying position. This
commercial review exists to tell the two apart, asset by asset, rather than assuming
either one by default.

Recoverability is typically affected by: vacancy and occupancy, participant funding,
provider position, operating costs, CAPEX and compliance, holding time and carry,
contractual friction, and transaction execution risk.

No single percentage applies to any of these — the effect is specific to the asset, not
a rule of thumb that transfers between matters.

Each of the four sources of impairment from page 14 tends to affect a different subset
of these factors. Mapping the two together — and testing which factors are genuinely
remediable — helps identify where value-protection effort may be most effective.
