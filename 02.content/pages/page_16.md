Before broad market launch or Buyer Due Diligence, the reader needs to know whether the
evidence underneath the asset is actually defensible — not just complete-looking. That
assessment runs across seven principal information categories that need to be
reconciled together, not checked off in isolation: **property; enrolment and
certification; participant funding and occupancy; provider and contracts; actual income
and operating costs; capital and compliance; and transaction structure.**

Missing or inconsistent information in any of these categories does not just slow a
process down — it becomes a value-at-risk issue once a buyer starts pricing the
uncertainty. Reconciling them together, rather than category by category, is where
Specialist SDA Commercial Vendor Due Diligence typically finds what a simpler review
misses.

A detailed appointment-specific information request will vary according to the
property, provider structure, participant/funding position and proposed realisation
pathway. This report therefore sets out the principal information categories rather
than the full diligence protocol. SDAHC maintains a more detailed appointment-specific
Vendor Due Diligence protocol.

This page is the audit. Whether you should actually launch, and in what order, is page
20's question.
