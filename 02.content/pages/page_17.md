# PAGE 17 — ONE PORTFOLIO OR SEVERAL TRANSACTIONS?

**Reader question:** What should I do next?

**Round 2 renumbering note:** was page 16 — shifts to page 17. Content unchanged besides
one cross-reference renumbering. See `../../00.project/changelog.md` for the full
old→new page map.

A portfolio does not have to be sold the way it was financed or owned. Packaging is a
recovery decision, not a marketing format — the choice affects achievable value, the
buyer universe you reach, and execution risk.

Buyer universe and achievable value can depend on considerably more than geography and
provider structure alone. Eleven factors are worth testing before a packaging decision
is made: SDA design category; building type; fire-sprinkler configuration; remaining
SDA pricing tenure (page 13); participant and funding profile; asset operating state
(stabilised versus distressed); provider structure; alternative-use value; geographic
or SA4 location; buyer or fund mandate; and portfolio size and homogeneity.

Institutional and fund mandates can differ materially, without any named buyer being
identifiable from the pattern: some funds target specific design categories, while
others will consider multiple or all categories; some require or prefer Class 3 or
sprinkler-configured stock; some exclude particular SA4 markets; and underwriting or
discounted-cash-flow assumptions can vary by geography. These are examples of market
segmentation dynamics, not a named buyer's mandate, a ranking of buyers, or SDAHC's
own scoring or matching logic — none of that is published here.

Bundling may improve execution in some circumstances, while in others it can obscure
asset-specific risk or narrow the buyer universe. A stronger asset may sometimes
support the marketability of a weaker but complementary one; conversely, a strong and
weak asset may simply have different buyer universes. The right answer depends on
execution certainty and recovery — not on how the portfolio presents on a summary
schedule.
