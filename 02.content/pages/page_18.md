# PAGE 18 — STABILISE, HOLD OR SELL?

**Reader question:** Is time creating value or destroying it?

There is no universally correct answer to whether time helps or hurts recovery.
Stabilisation can create value — closing an occupancy gap, completing a provider
transition, finishing outstanding CAPEX — or it can destroy it, through carry cost,
continued vacancy and drift.

The decision comes down to balancing recovery potential against the cost of delay: how
much recoverable improvement exists relative to the Current Recovery Position (page 13),
how long that takes, what it costs to hold, and which source of impairment (page 14) is
actually driving the distress. A provider-contractual problem with a credible transition
plan is a different holding decision from a fundamental asset problem with no realistic
fix.

**SDA remains a complex and relatively nascent asset class. Every property or portfolio
therefore warrants an asset-specific level of commercial diligence before the
realisation strategy is fixed.** Taking an asset to market through a conventional
process before Vendor Due Diligence may cause remediable SDA-specific value drivers to
be priced as uncertainty rather than resolved before sale — this is a caution about
process sequencing, not a claim that a conventional sale will fail or will achieve
materially less value.

A sequential rule — "fix everything, then decide" — is not always the right posture.
Sometimes the clock is the biggest risk in the file.
